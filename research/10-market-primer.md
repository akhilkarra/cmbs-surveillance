## If you remember nothing else

1. **CMBS = bonds backed by commercial mortgages.** Cash flows run through a sequential waterfall: AAA gets paid first and losses hit the bottom (B-piece) first. Deals are **conduits** (diversified pools of ~30–80 loans), **SASB** (one big loan or portfolio), **CRE CLOs** (managed or static pools of floating-rate bridge loans), or **agency** (Freddie K, Fannie DUS, Ginnie; multifamily, with a GSE guarantee on the senior part). [I]
2. **SASB is now the market.** It was $85.0B of $108.1B private-label CMBS YTD through 10/16/2026 (~79%). Conduit was $22.8B, and CRE CLO added another $38.0B (+64% YoY). [V](https://www.crefc.org/common/Uploaded%20files/Learn/CREFCFinanceData/2026%20MarketMetrics/CREFC%20MarketMetrics_10-16-26.pdf)
3. **2025 was the biggest post-GFC year:** $125.8B private-label CMBS (SASB $91.1B was a record) plus $30.6B CRE CLO, i.e. $156.4B total. Agency CMBS was another $159.2B. [V](https://www.crefc.org/common/Uploaded%20files/Learn/CREFCFinanceData/2026%20MarketMetrics/CREFC%20MarketMetrics_10-16-26.pdf)
4. **Size:** US CRE debt is **$6.5T** (2Q26). Banks hold **48%**, agencies 18%, life cos 12%, CMBS **10% (~$675B)**. [V](https://www.crefc.org/common/Uploaded%20files/Learn/CREFCFinanceData/2026%20MarketMetrics/CREFC%20MarketMetrics_10-16-26.pdf)
5. **Credit is still deteriorating.** The Trepp CMBS 30+ day delinquency rate was **8.02%** in Sep-2026, the highest since late 2020. Office was **12.16%**. The special-servicing rate was **11.49%**, the highest since Feb-2013. [V](https://www.scotsmanguide.com/news/cmbs-delinquencies-reach-highest-level-since-2020/) [V](https://www.credaily.com/?p=224030)
6. **Office is the epicenter**, but **multifamily (8.04%) now has a higher delinquency rate than retail or hotel.** Hotel delinquency has risen from 5.35% to 6.18% since July. [V](https://www.crefc.org/common/Uploaded%20files/Learn/CREFCFinanceData/2026%20MarketMetrics/CREFC%20MarketMetrics_10-16-26.pdf)
7. **The macro backdrop is a rate shock.** The 10y UST was **5.07%** on 10/16/2026, versus 3.97% a year earlier. That makes refinancing loans written at 3–4% coupons hard. $875B of CRE loans mature in 2026, $200B of them in CMBS and CRE CLOs. [V](https://www.crefc.org/common/Uploaded%20files/Learn/CREFCFinanceData/2026%20MarketMetrics/CREFC%20MarketMetrics_10-16-26.pdf)
8. **Spreads (early Sep-2026, CREFC):** conduit AAA **+70**, AA +130, A +175, BBB- **+415**. BofA had AAA at 75 and BBB- at 435 on 10/9/2026. [V\*](https://www.crefc.org/cre/cre/content/News/Items/Research_and_Data/2026/CRE_Securitized_Debt_Update_9_8_26.aspx) [V](https://finvaulta.com/research/bank-of-america/6-is-the-new-5-higher-treasury-yields-weigh-on-issuance-2026-10-09)
9. **CMBX** is the synthetic index (CDS on 25 conduit tranches per series), used to hedge and to take macro shorts. **Series 19 rolled Jan-2026**; its BBB- coupon is 300 bp. [V\*](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/index-news-and-announcements/CMBX.NA.BBB-.19.pdf)

## 1. What CMBS is, and the deal types

**One paragraph.** A lender (bank or non-bank) makes fixed- or floating-rate first-mortgage loans on income-producing property: office, hotel, retail, multifamily, industrial and so on. A depositor sells the loans into a REMIC trust, and the trust issues **tranches** of bonds rated AAA down to unrated. Each month, borrowers' payments flow through a **waterfall**. Interest goes to each class in order. Principal goes sequentially, AAA first. **Losses are written off from the bottom up.** A **master servicer** collects payments and advances. A **special servicer** works out loans that default. The most junior holder (the **B-piece / controlling class**) has rights to direct the special servicer and usually holds the 5% **risk-retention** slice. Investors pick the risk/return point they want in the capital stack. [I]

| Deal type | What's in it | Share / size (latest) |
|---|---|---|
| **Conduit** | 30–80 fixed-rate loans from several originators, diversified by property type and geography. The ~10-year term is being displaced by **5-year shelves (BANK5, Benchmark "V", BBCMS "5C", WFCM "5C", BMO "5C")** [V](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2104049/000153949726000292/exh4_5-v20psa.htm) | $22.8B YTD 10/16/26 (21% of PL CMBS); $33.7B in 2025 [V](https://www.crefc.org/common/Uploaded%20files/Learn/CREFCFinanceData/2026%20MarketMetrics/CREFC%20MarketMetrics_10-16-26.pdf). By mid-2026, 10-year loans were only 12.3% of new conduit loan count and 19.4% of balance (Trepp) [V](https://www.credaily.com/?p=219425) |
| **SASB** | One large loan (or a portfolio with one sponsor), often **floating rate (SOFR + spread)** with an interest-rate cap. Common for hotels, industrial portfolios, trophy office and data centers | $85.0B YTD (79% of PL CMBS); $91.1B in 2025, a record [V](https://www.crefc.org/common/Uploaded%20files/Learn/CREFCFinanceData/2026%20MarketMetrics/CREFC%20MarketMetrics_10-16-26.pdf) |
| **CRE CLO** | Floating-rate transitional/bridge loans, often multifamily. Static or managed, with reinvestment | $38.0B YTD (+64% YoY); $30.6B in 2025 [V](https://www.crefc.org/common/Uploaded%20files/Learn/CREFCFinanceData/2026%20MarketMetrics/CREFC%20MarketMetrics_10-16-26.pdf) |
| **Agency CMBS** | Freddie Mac **K-series** (Freddie guarantees the senior classes; the unguaranteed subordinate classes are sold to private B-piece buyers), Fannie Mae **DUS** MBS (usually one loan per pool), Ginnie Mae project loans | $128.4B YTD (Fannie $47.3B, Freddie $66.9B, Ginnie $14.2B); $159.2B in 2025 [V](https://www.crefc.org/common/Uploaded%20files/Learn/CREFCFinanceData/2026%20MarketMetrics/CREFC%20MarketMetrics_10-16-26.pdf) |

- [I] Shares of total private-label CRE securitization YTD 10/16/26, computed from the CREFC table: SASB 58%, CRE CLO 26%, conduit 16%. In 2025 the split was SASB 58%, conduit 22%, CRE CLO 20%.
- [I] The **5-year conduit** exists because borrowers didn't want to lock in 10-year money at high rates. For investors this means shorter WAL, quicker refinancing tests, and more extension risk if 2026 rates stay high.

### Maturity wall and the 2023–26 office story
- [V] **The wall.** $875B of CRE mortgages mature in 2026: banks $396B, CMBS + CRE CLO $200B, debt funds/REITs $163B, life cos $76B, agencies $39B. Another $652B matures in 2027. [V](https://www.crefc.org/common/Uploaded%20files/Learn/CREFCFinanceData/2026%20MarketMetrics/CREFC%20MarketMetrics_10-16-26.pdf)
  - For CMBS specifically, estimates vary by definition. One Trepp-based count was ~$65B left to mature by year-end 2026 as of August, of which $37B are hard maturities with no extensions left. [V](https://www.credaily.com/?p=221046)
- [V] **Refinancing math is the problem, not the volume of maturities.**
  - In a Trepp sample, more than half of maturing balances needed fresh equity to refinance. **~80% of IO loans fell short.**
  - Loans that failed to pay off had debt yields of ~9%, versus 13–14% for loans that paid off. [V\*](https://www.credaily.com/?p=217769)
  - BofA reports 2026 YTD payoff rates of 75% for SASB and 67% for conduit (through September). [V](https://finvaulta.com/research/bank-of-america/6-is-the-new-5-higher-treasury-yields-weigh-on-issuance-2026-10-09)
- [V] **Office.**
  - Office delinquency hit an all-time high of **12.34% in Jan-2026** [V](https://www.crefc.org/common/Uploaded%20files/Learn/CREFCFinanceData/2026%20MarketMetrics/CREFC%20MarketMetrics%20(4-24-26).pdf) and is back at 12.16%.
  - Office special servicing is 16.9%, and large CBD office loans transferred to special servicing in Aug-2026. [V](https://www.credaily.com/?p=222831)
  - KBRA's office distress rate rose from 8.6% (YE2023) to 17.4% (Oct-2025). [V](https://www.kbra.com/publications/dncMHcjj/kbra-releases-research-2026-u-s-cmbs-outlook-issuance-momentum-builds-loan-distress-remains-elevated?format=web)
- [I] **The arc.** Post-COVID remote work cut office demand (2021–22). Then the Fed's 2022–23 hikes broke refinancing for 10-year loans written in 2015–16. Special servicers turned to modifications and extensions ("extend and pretend"), so losses were deferred rather than avoided. Appraisal reductions and liquidations are now pushing losses into mezzanine (AS/AA/A) classes of 2.0-era conduits. Meanwhile, trophy/Class A office SASB is issuing again ($17.3B office YTD through July).
- [I] **New risks in 2026:** multifamily (bridge loans from the 2021–22 vintage, in CRE CLOs and Freddie K) and hotel. **The 10y moving above 5% reopens the refinancing gap** that tighter spreads had partly closed.

## 4. Liquidity and trading

- [I] **Market structure.**
  - CMBS trade **OTC, dealer-intermediated**. Most investment-grade cash bonds are 144A or public. Dealers show axes and run trades.
  - Larger or legacy blocks trade via **BWIC** (bid-wanted-in-competition: a seller circulates a list, dealers bid by a deadline, results are "colored" afterward) or OWIC (offer lists).
  - Pricing is quoted as a **spread to swaps or Treasuries** (fixed-rate conduit), **discount margin over SOFR** (floaters), or **price** (distressed or IO).
- [V] **FINRA TRACE.**
  - Non-agency CMBS is reported in the **ABSX** bucket together with CLOs/CDOs/CBOs.
  - **Sep-2026: ABSX averaged $5.83B par/day across 707.5 trades/day**; customer trades were $5.65B of that. [V](https://cdn.finra.org/trace/volume/monthly/TRACE_Public_Monthly_Report_2026-09.xlsx) (legend: [V](https://www.finra.org/finra-data/browse-catalog/trace-volume-reports/about-trace-monthly-volume-reports))
  - The FINRA–ICE **STAR** daily report isolates CMBS. **On 10/23/2026, non-agency CMBS** traded as follows:
    - IG P&I: 185 trades, $498M
    - HY P&I: 56 trades, $191M
    - IG IO: 22 trades, $760M current notional
  - Agency CMBS P&I on the same day: 131 trades, ~$326M. [V](https://cdn.finra.org/trace/FINRA_IDS_STAR.xlsx) (one-day snapshot, file dated as of 10/23/2026)
  - [I] So non-agency CMBS is roughly **~250–300 P&I trades/day and ~$0.7B of P&I balance/day**. That is small next to agency MBS, which is the reason relative-value and inventory-sourcing skills matter.

- **CMBX.**
  - [V\*] **What it is.** CMBX is a family of indices of **credit default swaps on 25 reference CMBS conduit tranches** per series.
    - Post-2011 series have six sub-indices: **AAA, AS, AA, A, BBB-, BB**.
    - Administered by S&P DJI, inherited from Markit/IHS. [V\*](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/methodologies/methodology-sp-cmbx-indices.pdf)
  - [V\*] **Series.** **CMBX 18** began trading 27-Jan-2025 [V\*](https://spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/index-news-and-announcements/20241224-cmbx-series-18-roll-timeline.pdf). **CMBX 19** rolled 26-Jan-2026 [V\*](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/indexnews/announcements/20260120-1482002/1482002_cmbxseries19staticdata20january2026.pdf); BBB-.19 has a 300 bp fixed coupon and a legal final of 17-Dec-2058 [V\*](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/index-news-and-announcements/CMBX.NA.BBB-.19.pdf). [I] Each series references deals from roughly one vintage, and new series now appear about once a year.
  - [I] **Mechanics.** It is pay-as-you-go: the protection seller pays the buyer for writedowns and interest shortfalls on the reference bonds, as they happen. The fixed coupon plus an upfront price settles the trade.
  - **Uses:**
    - [I] **Hedge** cash conduit mezzanine/BBB- inventory, since it is faster and cheaper than selling bonds.
    - [I] **Macro short** CRE. [V\*] The CMBX.6 retail/mall short of 2017–20 was the famous example (Canyon reportedly had a ~$1B BBB position) [V\*](https://www.alternativeswatch.com/2020/04/16/more-funds-shorting-cmbs-credit-stress-as-long-investors-return/), and AllianceBernstein published a counter-view [V\*](https://www.alliancebernstein.com/content/dam/global/insights/insights-whitepapers/Final_INS-7856-1019.pdf).
    - [I] **Basis/relative value** of CMBX against the cash bonds in the reference pools.
  - **CMBX liquidity:** I found no current public volume figure. DTCC/SDR public data would be the source. → *Open question.* [I] BBB- and BB on recent series are the most actively traded sub-indices.

## 6. Where a structured-credit hedge fund sits

[I] **What hedge funds and structured-credit specialists do in CMBS:**
1. **Relative value in mezzanine.** Pick specific BBB-/BB conduit bonds or SASB floating-rate mezzanine classes using loan-level views (property cash flows, sponsor, servicer behavior), and short the weaker names or CMBX.
2. **IO and "rake" bonds.** These are a bet on prepayment, defeasance and modification behavior.
3. **CMBX** as a hedge, a macro short, or a basis trade against cash.
4. **Distressed / special situations.** Legacy 2.0 conduit tails, controlling-class strategies, and loan/note purchases from special servicers.
5. **Liquidity provision.** Be the bid on BWICs when banks and insurers are forced sellers.

The desk-quant edge is in loan-level surveillance, cash-flow and waterfall modeling under default/extension/loss scenarios, and fast reaction to remittance and servicer news.

## 7. Glossary (one line each)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Tranche / class | One slice of the deal's bonds, with its own rating, coupon and priority. |
| Attachment / detachment | The loss levels (% of pool) at which a tranche starts and finishes taking losses. |
| Credit enhancement (CE) | Subordination beneath a tranche; e.g. 30% CE means the pool can lose 30% before the bond does. |
| WAL | Weighted-average life: the average time until principal is returned. |
| IO / X class | An interest-only strip paid from excess loan interest on a notional balance. It loses value with prepayment, default or modification. |
| Rake bond | Loan-specific classes (e.g. HCD/HCE) backed only by one loan's subordinate piece within a conduit. |
| Pari passu note | A large loan split into equal-priority notes spread across several deals. A co-lender agreement governs them. |
| A/B note | A senior note (A, in the trust) and a junior note (B, held outside or as a rake), with B taking losses first. |
| Defeasance | The borrower swaps the mortgage collateral for Treasuries to prepay. The bond keeps its cash flows. |
| Yield maintenance | A prepayment penalty that makes the lender whole at a Treasury-based discount rate. |
| NOI | Net operating income = property revenue minus operating expenses, before debt service. |
| DSCR | NOI (or NCF) / debt service; below 1.0x means the property can't cover its payments. |
| LTV | Loan / appraised value. |
| Debt yield | NOI / loan balance. The key refinancing metric; ~9% is weak and 13%+ is healthy in 2026 (per the Trepp data above). |
| Controlling class / directing holder | The most junior outstanding class (usually the B-piece buyer). It directs and can replace the special servicer. |
| B-piece | The unrated/BB/B bottom of a conduit, bought by specialist funds; often the risk-retention holder. |
| Risk retention | The rule (Dodd-Frank) requiring 5% of credit risk to be retained, typically via an eligible horizontal interest held by the B-piece buyer. |
| Master servicer | Collects payments, makes P&I and servicing **advances**, and handles performing loans. |
| Special servicer | Works out defaulted or imminent-default loans: modify, extend, foreclose, sell. |
| Special servicing transfer | When a loan moves from the master to the special servicer after a default trigger. |
| Watchlist | The servicer's list of performing loans showing stress (low DSCR, tenant loss, upcoming maturity). |
| Appraisal reduction amount (ARA) | A balance cut based on a new appraisal that reduces advances and shifts voting control downward. |
| ASER | Appraisal subordinate entitlement reduction: an interest shortfall that hits the bottom classes when an ARA exists. |
| Modification | Changed loan terms (rate, amortization, maturity, A/B split) to avoid foreclosure. |
| Extension | A maturity pushed out, either by a borrower option (common in floaters) or by a servicer modification. |
| Maturity default | The loan stays current but can't refinance or repay at maturity. This is the 2025–26 office story. |
| Term default | A missed payment before maturity because cash flow fails. |
| Loss severity | Loss / loan balance at resolution. |
| Realized loss | The writedown actually applied to the bonds once a loan is liquidated. |
| REO | Real estate owned: the trust has foreclosed and now owns the property. |
| CREFC IRP | CREFC's Investor Reporting Package: the standard monthly loan, property and servicer files. |
| ABS-EE | SEC Reg AB II loan-level monthly data (XML) filed on EDGAR for public deals. |
| 10-D | The monthly distribution report filed with the SEC (remittance). |
| 424B / prospectus, Annex A | Offering documents. Annex A is the loan-level tape with each loan's terms and underwriting. |
| BWIC / OWIC | Bid-wanted / offers-wanted in competition: auction-style secondary trading lists. |
| CMBX | CDS index on 25 conduit tranches per series (AAA through BB). |
| Conduit | A multi-borrower, multi-lender diversified fixed-rate CMBS. |
| SASB | Single-asset/single-borrower CMBS: one loan or portfolio. |
| Floater (SOFR + spread) | A floating-rate bond/loan paying 1-month Term SOFR plus a margin. Typical of SASB and CRE CLOs. |
| Interest-rate cap agreement | A borrower-purchased cap required on floating-rate loans that limits SOFR exposure. Cap expiry and replacement cost are key 2026 risks. |
| Operating advisor | A third party that monitors the special servicer once the controlling class has eroded. |
| Interest shortfall / non-recoverability | Advances stop when the servicer deems them unrecoverable, so the junior classes miss coupons. |
